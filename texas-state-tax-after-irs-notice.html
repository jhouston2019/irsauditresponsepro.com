Texas and Your IRS Notice: Which Agency Is Writing?

Last updated 2026-11-02 · State tax follow-ups
Short answer

The IRS notice you hold is a federal matter, governed by the federal guides on this site. The Texas Comptroller of Public Accounts is a separate agency that administers state taxes and may write separately. Check any Comptroller letter and its official site on their own.

Which agency sent it?

The first question with any tax letter is who sent it. Look at the letterhead, the notice number and the return address. A letter with an IRS notice number is federal, and the federal guides here apply.

A letter from the Texas Comptroller is something different, even if it reaches you the same week.

About the Comptroller

Texas taxes are administered by the Texas Comptroller of Public Accounts (comptroller.texas.gov). Its taxes page lists several tax types, including sales and use, franchise, motor vehicle and property taxes.

Because the agency handles a range of taxes, a letter from it may be about something unrelated to your federal return. Read it to see which tax it names. This page makes no claims about which Texas taxes apply to you.

A small seller's example

Suppose you sell products online and receive a CP2000 about payment-platform income that does not match your return. Your federal steps are to read the notice, compare it to your records and answer by the date on it.

If you also have a Comptroller account for another reason, that account and its letters follow the Comptroller's instructions, not the IRS's.

Practical steps

A few habits keep two agencies from blurring together.

  • Keep copies of every letter and mark which agency sent it.
  • Use contact information from the agency's official site, not from a message.
  • Do not send federal documents to a state address or the other way round unless a letter tells you to.

Where the tool fits

For the federal side, IRS Audit Defense Pro is software that helps you organize your facts and draft a response letter for your federal matter. You review it and send it yourself. For anything the Comptroller asks, follow the Comptroller's own instructions.

Questions to bring to the agency

If a Comptroller letter does arrive, write down what it asks before you call or write back: the tax it names, the period it covers, the reference number and the response date. Having those four items in front of you makes any conversation shorter and clearer.

You can also compare the letter with the list of taxes on the Comptroller's taxes page to see which area it falls under. If it still does not make sense, contact the agency using details from its own site, and consider a licensed professional if the amounts or periods look unfamiliar. None of this changes what the IRS expects on your federal notice.

Frequently asked questions

Does the Texas Comptroller handle my federal notice?

No. Federal notices come from the IRS. The Comptroller is a state agency with its own letters.

Why might I hear from the Comptroller at all?

Only the agency can say. Its taxes page covers several tax types, so a letter may concern a tax unrelated to your federal return.

Which source should I trust for Texas information?

Use the official site, comptroller.texas.gov, typed in yourself. Be cautious about links in unexpected messages.

Can I use one reply for both agencies?

No. Respond to each agency using its own letter and instructions.
