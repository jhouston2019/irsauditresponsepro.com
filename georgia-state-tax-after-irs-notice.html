Georgia: What an IRS Notice Means for Your State Contact

Last updated 2026-11-02 · State tax follow-ups
Short answer

The federal rules in this site's guides apply to your IRS notice. The Georgia Department of Revenue is a separate agency that may contact you separately. Read each letter on its own, and confirm state details on the agency's official site.

The Georgia agency

Georgia's tax agency is the Georgia Department of Revenue (dor.georgia.gov). Its homepage states that Georgia has an individual income tax based on federal adjusted gross income.

Because the state figure starts from a federal figure, it is reasonable to ask the department how a federal change should be handled. This page does not say what the answer is.

Why separate still matters

Even when numbers are connected, the agencies are not. The IRS asks you to respond to its notice by its date, and the department sets out its own steps in its own letters and website.

This page does not provide Georgia rules, rates, forms or deadlines.

Start by working out which letter you are holding. An IRS notice shows a CP or LTR number in its upper right corner, and that number can be searched on IRS.gov. A state letter will carry the department's own name and reference details instead. If either one looks unfamiliar, confirm it through the agency's official site before acting on anything it says.

An example

A freelancer receives a notice that a client's reporting differs from the return. Step one is the federal reply: compare the client's form to the books, decide whether you agree, and respond as the notice directs.

Step two, if needed, is asking the department how it wants to hear about a change. Contact it through dor.georgia.gov.

In that example, the freelancer keeps a single folder for the year with the notice, the return, the client's form, invoices and bank deposits, in date order. Keeping the records together makes it easier to answer the IRS and, later, to explain the same facts to the department if it asks.

Staying safe

Be careful with unexpected calls, texts or emails. The IRS says it normally begins by mail, and it never asks for payment by gift card. If a message claims to be from the state, go to the agency's site yourself.

Next steps

Reply to the IRS by its date. Write down your questions for the state. Consider asking a licensed professional if the two seem linked for you.

When you mail your federal reply, keep a photocopy and a receipt. Federal law generally treats a timely postmarked item as timely, yet a postmark can land later than the day you dropped it off, so certified mail from a post office counter is a sensible choice. Do the same for anything you send the state, following the department's instructions.

If both agencies write about the same year, answer the earlier date first. A licensed representative can help read both letters together, and the IRS describes representation as available to you if you want it.

What to bring to a conversation with the department

If you decide to ask the Georgia Department of Revenue about a federal change, prepare first. Have your federal notice, your return, and a short summary of what you agreed or disagreed with. Know what you want to ask, such as whether anything further is needed from you and how the agency prefers to receive it.

Write down the answer and the name of whoever gave it. Then follow what the agency tells you, not what a general article suggests, including this one.

Frequently asked questions

Does Georgia have an income tax?

The Georgia Department of Revenue's homepage states Georgia has an individual income tax based on federal adjusted gross income. For anything more, see dor.georgia.gov.

Do I need to tell Georgia about a federal change?

This page cannot say. Ask the Georgia Department of Revenue or a licensed professional.

Is a state letter the same as an IRS notice?

No. They come from different agencies and have different instructions.

Can your tool answer the state?

It helps with your federal response letter and does not replace the state's own instructions.
