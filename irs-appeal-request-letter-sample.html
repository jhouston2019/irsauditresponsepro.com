IRS appeal request letter sample: asking for an Appeals conference

Last updated 2026-11-02 · Sample response letters
Short answer

An appeal request letter states that you want to appeal, identifies the letter and tax periods, lists each change you disagree with and the reason, and is signed. For a small case the IRS accepts a brief statement; larger cases need a formal protest. Send it to the address in your letter.

Is this the right letter?

Publication 5 explains the two ways to ask Appeals to review proposed audit changes. A small case request is available when total tax and penalties for each tax period in the letter are $25,000 or less. A formal written protest is required if any period exceeds that, and for employee plan, exempt organization, partnership and S corporation cases regardless of amount.

This sample is a small case request, written as a short letter. If your case needs a formal protest, you will also have to include the law or authority you rely on and a statement under penalties of perjury; the same structure applies but with more detail.

To decide, look in your letter for the tax and penalties shown for each period and compare them with the small case amount above. If any one period is over, the formal protest rules apply to all periods. If you cannot tell from the report, or the case involves a partnership or S corporation, read Publication 5 before drafting, because choosing the wrong format can cost time.

Dates and addresses come from your letter

The deadline is the date in the letter you received, and the IRS says the response time is generally 30 days from the letter date. Send the request to the address in the letter, not to Appeals directly. If the letter includes a form for a small case request, you can use it instead of or alongside this letter.

The example involves a married couple whose examiner disallowed a charitable deduction and a business mileage deduction; they accept the first and want Appeals to look at the second.

Put the deadline on your calendar as soon as the letter arrives, and plan to mail well before it. A short request that arrives on time is better than a complete one that arrives late, and your letter can offer to provide further records at a conference.

Writing the disagreement

Name each adjustment as the examination report names it. State whether you disagree with the whole amount or part of it, and give the reason in a sentence or two. Do not argue everything; a focused list is clearer than a general complaint.

Avoid tone that treats the examiner as an opponent. Appeals is a separate part of the IRS, and the reviewer will read your reasons fresh. Your documents carry the weight.

Keep a copy of the examination report beside your draft and check that each adjustment you list matches the report's wording and page. If you agree with some changes, say so in the letter; the IRS asks you to list the disputed issues, so a clear split between what you accept and what you contest keeps the request focused. For a joint return, both spouses generally sign.

For each disputed item, answer three questions in order: what did the examiner change, why do you think it is wrong, and what proves it. Use the same order for every item so the reviewer sees a pattern. If your reason depends on a record that is incomplete, say what the record shows and what it does not, instead of leaving the gap for the reviewer to find.

Supporting papers

The IRS says to include facts supporting your position. Typical enclosures:

Index your enclosures at the bottom of the letter and reference them by number in the text. If the same document supports two items, enclose it once and cite it twice. Make copies of everything and keep the originals; you may need them at a conference, where you can explain the records in person.

  • A copy of the letter proposing the changes and the examination report
  • Contemporaneous logs, such as a mileage log, for the disputed item
  • Statements, invoices or receipts the examiner did not have
  • Any written exchange with the examiner about the item

After you send it

Appeals will contact you to discuss the case. If you do not reach an agreement, the IRS may issue a notice of deficiency, which carries a 90-day period to petition the Tax Court, or 150 days if addressed outside the United States; check your notice. Frequently asked questions

Can I use this letter for a formal protest?

Not as written. A formal protest has additional required elements, including the law or authority you rely on and a penalties-of-perjury statement, according to Publication 5.

Who should I address it to?

Use the address and contact in your letter. The IRS says to send the request there, not to Appeals directly.

Does appealing hurt my right to go to court?

The IRS says using Appeals is optional and does not waive the right to go to court.

Do I need a representative?

No. You may represent yourself or use an authorized representative.

What if my case is over the small case amount?

Then a formal written protest is required, covering all periods in the letter.
