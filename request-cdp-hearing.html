How to request a Collection Due Process hearing

Last updated 2026-11-02 · How-to guides
Short answer

Find the Collection Due Process notice, note the deadline printed on it (generally about 30 days), complete Form 12153 with your reasons, attach what applies, and send it to the hearing address on the notice. A timely request is heard by the IRS Independent Office of Appeals.

Before you start

A Collection Due Process hearing, or CDP hearing, is your chance to have the Independent Office of Appeals review certain collection actions before or soon after they happen. It applies to specific notices: a notice of federal tax lien filing, a final notice of intent to levy, and a few others. Not every collection letter qualifies, so confirm your notice names hearing rights.

The deadline is the number that matters most. The IRS has described the levy request period differently across its materials, as running from the notice date in some places and from receipt in others, so rely on the date printed on your own notice and treat it as firm. Statute-based timing for a levy hearing is 30 days, and a lien notice also gives 30 days.

What a hearing can cover

Appeals considers collection alternatives such as a payment plan or an offer, and it can look at the underlying liability only if you did not have an earlier chance to dispute it. A timely request generally stops levy action and suspends the collection time limit until the decision is final.

One hearing is held per tax period. If a lien and a levy involve the same period, the lien procedure is coordinated with the levy procedure.

Late requests: the equivalent hearing

If you miss the deadline, you may still ask for an equivalent hearing. The IRS FAQ says that request must come within a year of the levy notice, or within a year plus a short additional period after a lien is filed. An equivalent hearing is a different procedure from a timely CDP hearing, so read the FAQ for what it does and does not provide.

Common snags

People often send Form 12153 to the payment address. The form goes to the hearing address on the CDP notice. Others leave the reason line blank, but the IRS requires a reason. A third problem is asking for a payment plan without the financial statements Appeals needs; if you propose an alternative, expect to attach Form 433-A or 433-B, whichever fits.

If Appeals issues a determination, you may petition the Tax Court within 30 days of that determination. The Tax Court is independent of the IRS, and the 30-day window is set by statute. Step by step

  1. Find your CDP notice and its dateIdentify the notice that mentions your right to a hearing. Note the date on it, the deadline it states, the tax periods covered and the hearing address.
  2. Decide what you want Appeals to considerCommon reasons include proposing a payment plan, questioning whether the lien or levy is appropriate, or disputing the amount when you had no earlier chance to do so. Write them in plain sentences.
  3. Download and complete Form 12153Use the current version from the IRS site. Enter your information, the notice type and the periods, and give your reason for the hearing. The reason is required.
  4. Attach what appliesInclude a copy of the notice. If you are proposing an alternative, attach the financial statement the IRS asks for. If you are seeking innocent spouse relief, attach Form 8857. If a representative signs for you, attach Form 2848.
  5. Mail to the hearing address, not the payment addressUse the address named for hearing requests on the CDP notice. Send by certified mail or another method with a receipt, and mail it early enough that a late postmark cannot cause a problem.
  6. Keep proof and a full copyFile the receipt with a copy of everything you sent. Note the date.
  7. Prepare for the Appeals contactAppeals will contact you to schedule a conference. Have your financial information and any payment proposal ready.
  8. Review the determinationRead the written determination when it arrives and note the date. If you disagree, the Tax Court petition period is short, so act promptly.

Frequently asked questions

Is the CDP hearing the same as an audit appeal?

No. A CDP hearing concerns collection actions like liens and levies. Audit appeals concern examination results and usually follow a different letter.

Do I need a financial statement?

If you propose an alternative such as a payment plan, Appeals generally needs financial information. The form instructions say which statement fits your situation.

Can I request a hearing online?

Form 12153 is mailed or sent as your notice directs. Check the notice for the current method.

What if the deadline has passed?

Ask about an equivalent hearing promptly, and read the IRS FAQ for its time limit and what it does and does not do.

Can I ask for a different kind of appeal instead?

The Collection Appeals Program covers a broader set of actions and is generally faster, but it cannot challenge the liability and cannot go to court. Form 9423 requests it.
