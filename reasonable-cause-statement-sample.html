Reasonable cause statement sample: telling the story of what happened

Last updated 2026-11-02 · Sample response letters
Short answer

A reasonable cause statement explains, in dated order, what prevented you from meeting a requirement, how you tried to act with ordinary care and prudence, and what you did once the problem ended. It names the penalty, stays factual, and lists the documents that support each point.

What a reasonable cause statement does

The IRS may remove or reduce certain penalties when you acted in good faith and with reasonable cause, and it decides case by case. The statement is where you supply the facts. For late filing or payment, the IRS looks at whether you used ordinary care and prudence. Its page lists examples such as fire or disaster, inability to obtain records, serious illness or death in the family, and system problems.

The same page says that reliance on a tax professional, lack of knowledge, mistakes, and lack of funds alone generally do not qualify. Knowing that helps you decide what to emphasize: not what you did not know, but what actually stood in the way and how you responded.

The shape of a good statement

Reviewers read many of these. The ones that are easiest to follow set out a timeline: what the requirement was, what happened and when, what you did during the problem, and what you did when it ended. Each statement of fact should point to a document.

The sample below uses a self-employed person whose records and computer were unavailable after a flood at the home office, and who then filed as soon as replacement records arrived. Your facts will differ, so use the structure, not the story.

Writing it in your own words

Use first person and past tense. Avoid adjectives about how hard it was; describe the event and its effect. Include the dates, the people you contacted, and the steps you took to comply while the problem lasted.

Do not stretch the facts to fit the list of examples. If the real reason was a mistake or being short of funds, say what happened plainly and add whatever circumstances surrounded it. The IRS also says reasonable cause does not apply to the estimated tax penalty.

Documents that help

Match the documents to the event you describe.

  • Insurance claim or disaster assistance papers for a loss
  • Letters from a hospital or physician, with sensitive details limited to what is needed
  • Records of requests you made for lost or unavailable documents
  • Screenshots or error messages if a system problem blocked you
  • The filing or payment confirmation showing when you complied

Where it goes

The IRS says to call the number on your notice first, with the notice and facts ready, and to use Form 843 or a written request if the call cannot resolve it. This statement can be attached to Form 843 or sent as a letter. A denied failure-to-file or failure-to-pay request can be appealed using the instructions in the denial letter. Background. In the period before the due date I [DESCRIBE YOUR NORMAL PRACTICE, FOR EXAMPLE: GATHERED MY RECORDS AND PREPARED MY RETURN EACH YEAR IN ADVANCE].

What happened. On or about [DATE OF EVENT], [DESCRIBE THE EVENT IN PLAIN TERMS]. As a result, [DESCRIBE HOW IT PREVENTED YOU FROM FILING OR PAYING]. See enclosure 1.

What I did during that time. On [DATE] I [DESCRIBE STEP, FOR EXAMPLE: CONTACTED THE PAYER TO REQUEST REPLACEMENT RECORDS]. On [DATE] I [DESCRIBE NEXT STEP]. See enclosure 2.

When the problem ended. On or about [DATE], [DESCRIBE WHAT CHANGED, FOR EXAMPLE: I RECEIVED THE REPLACEMENT RECORDS]. I [FILED OR PAID] on [DATE]. See enclosure 3.

I took these steps because I intended to comply with my obligations, and the delay was caused by the circumstances described, not by indifference. I would appreciate a written reply. If you need more information, I can be reached at [DAYTIME PHONE].

I declare that the statements above are true to the best of my knowledge.

Sincerely,

Frequently asked questions

Is reasonable cause the same as first-time abatement?

No. First-time abatement is based on your compliance history. Reasonable cause is based on the circumstances of this particular failure. The IRS treats reasonable cause as the fallback when administrative relief does not apply.

Does a mistake by my preparer count?

The IRS page says reliance on a tax professional generally does not qualify by itself. Describe the facts and check the IRS page.

How much detail should I give about an illness?

Give enough to show the timing and effect, and limit private medical detail to what the documents need to show.

Can I use the statement for any penalty?

It can support requests for certain penalties the IRS lists, such as failure to file or pay, failure to deposit, accuracy-related and information return penalties. It does not apply to the estimated tax penalty.

Should I sign under penalty of perjury?

A signed statement that the facts are true is sensible. If you attach it to a form, follow that form's instructions on signing.
