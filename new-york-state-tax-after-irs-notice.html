An IRS Notice in New York: Keeping Federal and State Mail Straight

Last updated 2026-11-02 · State tax follow-ups
Short answer

The IRS notice you received follows federal rules covered in our federal guides. The New York Department of Taxation and Finance is a separate agency that may contact you separately, so read any state letter on its own and confirm details on the agency's official site.

What this page covers

It covers one narrow question: you are in New York, you have an IRS notice, and you want to know how the state tax agency fits in. The short answer is that it is a different office with different paperwork.

Everything about your federal notice, such as what the IRS is asking and when it wants a reply, is explained in the federal guides on this site.

A retiree's example

Picture a retiree who gets an IRS letter questioning how a pension distribution was reported. The first job is to read the federal letter, find its response date and gather the Form 1099-R and the return.

If a separate letter later comes from New York, it is a separate matter to read, not an extension of the federal one.

In practice, the retiree would set the pension statement beside the filed return and check whether the amounts line up. The IRS describes a notice like this as a proposed change that may increase, decrease or leave the tax unchanged, and it says the notice is not a bill.

The New York agency

New York's tax agency is the Department of Taxation and Finance (tax.ny.gov). Its site is the place to look for how the agency wants to be contacted and what its letters mean.

This page does not describe New York tax rules, deadlines or reporting requirements. Those belong to the agency, and details change.

Your options for sorting it out

You do not have to guess about overlap.

If both agencies write about the same year, put the two response dates side by side on a calendar and start with whichever comes first.

  • Reply to the IRS by the date on your federal notice.
  • Read any New York letter in full and note who sent it.
  • Look up the agency on tax.ny.gov, and use contact details you found there.
  • Ask a licensed professional if the two matters seem tangled.

Verify before you act

Scammers imitate agencies. The IRS says unexpected, threatening or payment-demanding contacts deserve suspicion. Apply the same caution to a message that claims to be from the state.

The notice number appears in the upper right corner of an IRS letter, and the IRS site can be searched by that number. The IRS also says it usually makes first contact by mail, and that it emails or texts only if you opted in. A direct message on social media is never from the IRS. If a state letter or message looks odd, type tax.ny.gov into your browser yourself instead of following a link or phone number printed in the message.

How the tool helps

The tool helps you gather your facts and draft a response letter for the federal matter. You edit, sign and send it yourself. It does not take the place of reading the state agency's own instructions.

Think of it as a way to keep your own paperwork in order. You describe what the notice says, line up the documents that support your position, and review the draft before anything leaves your hands.

Copies and proof of mailing

Whatever you send, keep a full copy first. If you mail it, the IRS-related guidance we rely on points to certified mail or another method that gives you a receipt, and to keeping that receipt with your copy. The National Taxpayer Advocate has noted that a postmark can reflect processing time rather than the day you dropped the envelope, so going to a post office counter is safer than a curbside box.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to contact New York because I got an IRS notice?

That is a question for the New York Department of Taxation and Finance. This page cannot say. Check tax.ny.gov or any letter you received from the state.

Is the Department of Taxation and Finance part of the IRS?

No. It is New York's own tax agency. The IRS is the federal agency, and each has separate letters and instructions.

What if the dates on the federal and state letters differ?

Follow the date on each letter for that agency. Do not assume one reply covers both.

Does this page give New York tax advice?

No. It points you to the agency's official site and to the federal guides. For advice on your situation, speak to a licensed professional.
