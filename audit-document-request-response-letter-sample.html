Cover letter for answering an IRS audit document request

Last updated 2026-11-02 · Sample response letters
Short answer

An audit response cover letter references the IRS's request, answers each numbered item in order, lists the photocopies you are sending, asks the examiner to confirm receipt, and gives a contact. Send copies rather than originals, and reply by the date in the request.

Where this letter fits in a mail audit

In an audit by mail, the IRS writes to ask for documents that support items on your return. IRS Publication 3498-A explains the process, including the phone and in-person conferences that can follow. The publication says to send photocopies, not originals, and that if you do not reply by the due date, the IRS disallows the items in question.

This sample is a straightforward cover letter, the kind you would send with a folder of records. It is written in a businesslike, neutral register: no argument, no narrative, just an organized answer. Think of a small contractor responding to a request about vehicle and supply expenses, where the whole point is that the examiner can find each document quickly.

The letter works best when the request is specific. If the IRS asked only for records, answer only with records. If the request asks you to explain something, add a short factual explanation under the matching item rather than a separate narrative at the start.

Preparing the package

Go through the request line by line and number your enclosures to match its numbered items. If the request asks about three items, your folder should have three clearly labeled tabs. Gaps are better acknowledged than hidden: if a record no longer exists, say so in the letter and enclose whatever substitute you have.

Keep a duplicate of the complete package, in the same order, so you can talk through it on the phone if the examiner calls. Write the examiner's name, the date you mailed the package and the tracking or receipt number on the first page of your file copy, so a later call does not depend on memory.

Avoid sending more than was requested. Extra pages tend to raise new questions, and a tidy response to exactly what was asked is easier for everyone to review.

Read each document before you copy it. Check that the name, account and dates shown are legible, and that pages are in order and complete, including back sides that carry information. A short, readable statement is more useful than a stack of poor scans. If a document includes unrelated information, such as a long list of personal purchases on a card statement, ask a qualified practitioner or call the number on the request before deciding what to do about it.

Edit this letter before sending

Replace the bracketed placeholders and delete any paragraph that does not apply. Keep the description of each document short and factual; the documents should do the talking.

If you want a representative to deal with the IRS for you, Publication 3498-A says you may authorize an attorney, CPA or enrolled agent. It also says that if you ask to consult a representative during an interview, the IRS generally must stop and reschedule. If a representative is involved, say so in the letter and enclose the proper authorization form.

Take the sample paragraph by paragraph. The opening ties your reply to the request, so confirm the date and reference match the letter you have. The item paragraphs should echo the request's own wording. The missing-record paragraph is for honest gaps only; delete it if everything was found. The closing paragraph keeps the door open for follow-up and asks for confirmation of receipt, which is useful if the examiner later says something was not received.

Enclosure checklist

Common categories to consider, depending on what the IRS asked for:

  • Bank or card statements covering the periods in question
  • Receipts or invoices, sorted by item number
  • Mileage or time logs created at the time
  • Contracts, agreements or written correspondence
  • Third-party statements, such as from a payer or landlord
  • A copy of the request letter on top

Mailing and follow-up

Use a mailing method that produces a receipt, and note the date. If you have not heard back after a reasonable time, call the number on the request to confirm the package was received. Send the package to the address named in the request, and if your request offers fax or an online option, follow what it says. After mailing, keep every later letter in the same folder in date order. If the examiner replies with further questions, answer them in a new, short letter that refers to the earlier one, rather than resending the whole package.

Frequently asked questions

Should I send originals?

No. Publication 3498-A says to send photocopies. Keep your originals.

What happens if I do not reply?

Publication 3498-A says that if there is no reply by the due date, the IRS disallows the items in question.

Can the IRS contact other people about my return?

The publication says third parties such as banks or employers may be contacted, and that you can ask for a list of those contacted.

Can someone else handle this for me?

You may authorize an attorney, CPA or enrolled agent. At Appeals conferences, those are the three kinds of representative the publication mentions.

Should I write more than a cover letter?

Only if the request asks for an explanation. Otherwise, keep the letter short and let the organized records answer the questions.
