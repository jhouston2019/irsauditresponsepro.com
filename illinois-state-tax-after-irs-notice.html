Illinois and Your IRS Notice: Two Agencies, Two Letters

Last updated 2026-11-02 · State tax follow-ups
Short answer

The federal rules in this site's guides apply to your IRS notice. The Illinois Department of Revenue is a separate agency and may contact you separately. Read each letter on its own and check tax.illinois.gov before relying on anything about the state side.

Order of operations

Start with the IRS letter. Find the notice type, the response date and the part that explains what changed. Our step-by-step guide on responding to an IRS notice sets out a sensible order.

Only after that is under control is it worth thinking about whether another agency has questions.

If a second agency writes about the same year, do not combine the replies. Answer the IRS first by its date, then read the state letter for its own date and sender. Writing both dates at the top of your log keeps either from slipping by.

The Illinois Department of Revenue

Illinois' tax agency is the Illinois Department of Revenue (tax.illinois.gov). Its homepage lists resources for individuals, including an income tax section for individuals and the IL-1040 form.

What the agency requires after a federal change is a question for that agency. This page does not state Illinois rules or deadlines.

A wage earner's example

Suppose an employee gets a CP2000 because a W-2 amount differs from what was on the return. The federal fix may be as simple as showing the W-2 you used, or agreeing and correcting the return. That decision rests on the federal notice.

If Illinois then sends a letter, the agency's own instructions and website are what count.

To prepare, the employee would pull the W-2 from the employer, the pay stubs for the year and the filed return. The IRS says a notice like this is a proposal, not a bill, and that you can agree or disagree by the response date. If you disagree, explain each item and attach support.

Questions to ask yourself

Use these before replying to anything.

If you cannot answer the first question from the letter itself, treat that as a reason to slow down. The IRS notes that the notice number sits in the upper right corner, and that its site can be searched by that number.

  • Which agency signed this letter?
  • What date does it give for a reply?
  • Did I find the agency's phone or web details on its own site?
  • Do I need help from a licensed professional?

Keeping a simple record

A one-page log is enough: the date each letter arrived, the sender, what it asked for, what you sent back and how you sent it. If you mail anything, use a method that gives you proof of delivery.

If you later speak with either agency, the log lets you answer questions quickly and avoid contradicting yourself. It also helps a professional, should you hire one, understand the timeline without sorting through a stack of envelopes.

Certified mail with a receipt is a sensible choice for anything with a deadline, since the postmark date controls and you carry the risk if it falls late.

Frequently asked questions

Does an IRS notice mean Illinois will contact me?

Not necessarily, and this page cannot predict it. Ask the Illinois Department of Revenue if you are unsure.

Is the Illinois Department of Revenue the same as the IRS?

No. It is the state's tax agency and operates separately from the federal agency.

What if I am not sure a letter is genuine?

The IRS suggests that unexpected, urgent or payment-demanding contacts deserve suspicion. Verify through tax.illinois.gov or IRS.gov, not via the letter's own links.

Should I send my federal response to Illinois?

Only if the state's letter tells you to. Otherwise send the federal reply to the IRS as the notice directs.
