New Jersey and the IRS: Handling a Balance Notice and State Mail

Last updated 2026-11-02 · State tax follow-ups
Short answer

Federal rules in this site's guides govern your IRS notice. The New Jersey Division of Taxation is a separate agency and may contact you separately. Read any state letter on its own, and use the Division's official pages for state questions.

Federal first

Say you receive an IRS notice showing the IRS changed your return and now shows a balance. The federal guides on this site, such as the CP11 and payment-plan pages, cover those steps.

Read the notice for the amount, the explanation and the date on it. That is the priority.

If an IRS balance notice and a state letter arrive close together, resist the urge to treat them as one problem. Put each in its own envelope or folder, mark the date it arrived, and find the notice number in the upper right corner of the federal one. The IRS says to review a notice carefully, keep it, and respond by the due date it states.

The Division of Taxation

New Jersey's tax agency is the Division of Taxation, part of the Department of the Treasury (nj.gov/treasury/taxation). Its Gross Income Tax page states that New Jersey levies a Gross Income Tax on resident and nonresident individuals, estates and trusts.

Beyond that, this page does not explain New Jersey rules, rates or deadlines. The Division is the place to ask.

What to keep apart

Keeping the matters distinct avoids mistakes.

For mail with a deadline, ask for a receipt. A timely postmark generally counts as timely under federal law, though postmarks can reflect processing time rather than drop-off day, so a post office counter and certified mail are safer than a mailbox. Keep the receipt with your copy of what you sent.

  • An IRS payment goes to the IRS through the options on IRS.gov.
  • A state payment goes to the state through its own channels.
  • Never send one agency's payment or paperwork to the other.

Check for scams

The IRS says it does not accept gift cards and that a demand for immediate payment is a warning sign. The same common sense applies if someone claims to be calling about state taxes. Go to the Division's website yourself.

Choosing your next step

If the IRS balance is correct, the federal guides explain payment plans and other ways to handle it. If you think the IRS is wrong, they explain how to respond in writing with your supporting records. Either way, do it by the date on the notice.

State questions can wait until the federal response is underway, unless a state letter has its own earlier date. In that case, look at both dates side by side and plan around the sooner one.

Whatever you choose, keep your own copy of the notice and any reply, and note the date you sent it. That record is useful if either agency asks what you did and when.

Frequently asked questions

Does New Jersey have an income tax?

The Division of Taxation page states that New Jersey levies a Gross Income Tax on resident and nonresident individuals, estates and trusts. For more, see the Division's site.

Will the Division of Taxation see my federal balance notice?

This page cannot say. Contact the Division through its official site.

Where do I pay the IRS?

Use the payment options listed on IRS.gov, not links in unsolicited messages.

Does this page cover state deadlines?

No. Deadlines for New Jersey come from the Division and from any letter it sends you.
