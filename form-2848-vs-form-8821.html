Form 2848 vs Form 8821: Represent or Just View?

Last updated 2026-11-02 · Guides and rights
Short answer

Form 2848 is a power of attorney that lets the person you name represent you before the IRS and receive your tax information, within the scope you list. Form 8821 only lets the person receive or inspect your information and gives no authority to represent you.

The difference in one minute

Both forms name another person and both involve your tax information, which is why they get confused. The dividing line is simple: authority to act versus permission to look.

  • Form 2848, Power of Attorney and Declaration of Representative: the named person may represent you before the IRS and receive your tax information, but only for the matters, forms and years you list.
  • Form 8821, Tax Information Authorization: the named person may receive or inspect your tax information. It does not let them speak for you, negotiate, or sign anything on your behalf.
  • If someone needs to talk to an examiner for you, attend a meeting or file a response in your place, that points to Form 2848.
  • If someone only needs to read your account, for example a lender, an accountant gathering numbers, or a family member helping you organize, that points to Form 8821.

Who can be named on Form 2848

A representative has to be someone the rules allow to practice before the IRS. Under Circular 230, attorneys and CPAs in good standing and enrolled agents may do so, and enrolled actuaries and enrolled retirement plan agents may do so in limited areas. You can always represent yourself, and the rules allow limited representation by some others, such as an immediate family member.

Preparers who are not attorneys, CPAs or enrolled agents have narrower rights. IRS Publication 947 describes them as able to appear only in limited settings, for returns they prepared and signed, and not before Appeals or revenue officers. It also states that Form 8821 will not authorize an unenrolled preparer to represent you. Filling out the wrong form does not widen anyone's authority.

Scope: what the form actually covers

Neither form is a blank check. Form 2848 is limited by the matters and years you write on it, so describe them carefully. A representative named for one income tax year does not automatically speak for another. Form 8821 is likewise limited to the information and periods you list.

If you and a spouse filed a joint return and both want a representative, each spouse signs a separate Form 2848. Sharing one form is not enough.

Choosing between them: three everyday cases

A freelance designer receives an audit letter and wants her enrolled agent to take over the correspondence. She needs Form 2848, because the agent must speak and sign for her.

A couple applying for a mortgage wants their loan officer to see transcripts. The lender only needs to view information, so Form 8821 fits, not Form 2848.

A small-business owner is happy to handle the response himself but wants his bookkeeper to pull account details. Form 8821 for the bookkeeper lets her look without any authority to act. Which form you need depends on the task, not on how much you trust the person.

Where forms fit with a drafted response

If you respond on your own, you usually do not need either form. If you later bring in a representative, the IRS notice or hearing request may ask you to attach a Form 2848 with the representative's signature, for example with a Collection Due Process hearing request.

Common snags

Details to double-check before you sign anything:

  • Read the current form instructions. The IRS updates forms, and Topic 311 on the IRS site is the starting point for how the forms work.
  • Confirm the credential of the person you are naming, and that it covers the type of matter you have.
  • List the right tax forms and years. Too narrow a description can mean the IRS will not talk to your representative about the notice in front of you.
  • Keep a copy of what you signed and know who holds the original.
  • Remember that signing authorizes access to your information, so name only people you trust.

Frequently asked questions

Can someone with only Form 8821 talk to the IRS for me?

No. Form 8821 lets the named person receive or inspect your tax information. It gives no authority to represent you, so use Form 2848 if you want someone to act for you.

Who can I name on a Form 2848?

Attorneys and CPAs in good standing and enrolled agents may represent you, as may some others in limited roles. You can also represent yourself. Check Circular 230 and IRS Publication 947 for what each type of person may do.

Do both spouses need to sign on a joint return?

If each spouse wants a representative on a joint return matter, each signs a separate Form 2848.

Do I need a Form 2848 to respond to a notice myself?

Generally no, because you are speaking for yourself. A form comes into play when someone else will act for you or needs to see your information.

Is a Form 2848 permanent?

The form limits authority to the matters and periods you list. Check the form instructions and the IRS Topic 311 page for the current rules on how long an authorization lasts and how to revoke it.
