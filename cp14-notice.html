What a CP14 notice means and what to do next

Last updated 2026-11-02 · IRS notices and letters
Short answer

A CP14 is the IRS balance-due notice. It tells you that you owe tax, shows an amount and a due date, and asks you to pay. If you cannot pay in full you can ask for a payment plan, and if you disagree you call the number on the notice.

What a CP14 is

A CP14 is usually the first bill you receive when the IRS has a balance on your account that was not paid with your return. It states what you owe, the tax period it relates to and the date by which to pay. It is a request for payment, not a seizure or a penalty notice in itself.

If you receive a version labeled CP14C because your address is in a federally declared disaster area, the IRS says extra time to file and pay may apply automatically. Look at the dates printed on your notice before assuming anything.

Why interest and penalties matter right away

The IRS says no interest is charged if you pay the full amount by the date on the notice. After that date, interest builds on whatever remains. It also says a late payment penalty applies if you do not pay in full, and that contacting the IRS or applying for a payment plan before the due date may help avoid or reduce it.

For background, the late-payment penalty is generally 0.5% of the unpaid tax for each month or part of a month, up to 25% of the unpaid tax. The IRS page describes it as lower while an approved payment plan is in effect and higher after a notice of intent to levy. Interest is reset every quarter and compounds daily, so check the IRS interest page for the current figure instead of relying on a number from another site.

Check the notice against your own records

Before paying, spend a few minutes confirming the notice makes sense. Compare the tax year with the return you filed, look for payments you made that may not be reflected, and see whether an amended return you filed could change the amount. A balance can also appear because the IRS adjusted something on your return, in which case the notice should say so.

If you made a payment very recently, it may not have posted yet. Keep your bank record or canceled check handy either way.

Your choices

Most readers land in one of three places:

  • Pay in full by the due date on the notice and stop interest from building.
  • Pay what you can and ask for a payment plan. Many individuals can apply online if they owe under the IRS threshold and have filed all returns; otherwise you can request a plan by Form 9465 or by phone. Setup fees vary by plan type, so check the IRS page.
  • Disagree. Call the number on the notice and have your support ready, such as canceled checks or a copy of an amended return.

If you agree but cannot pay

Responding is better than waiting. A payment plan does not remove interest, and penalties and interest continue until the balance is paid, but a plan keeps the account in good standing and reduces the chance of tougher collection notices later. Choose a monthly amount you can really sustain, because a plan you cannot keep up with creates a new problem.

If you disagree with the amount

Call the number on the notice first. If the problem is a mistake in the facts, such as a payment that was not credited, a short written explanation with copies of your records often makes your case clearer than a phone call alone. Keep the letter factual: the tax year, the amount you dispute, what you believe is correct and what is enclosed.

What happens if you do nothing

The IRS sends reminder notices when a balance stays open. Later notices describe stronger collection steps, which is why a quiet CP14 is easier to handle than the ones that follow it. Acting now keeps your options wide.

Frequently asked questions

Is a CP14 a final notice?

No. It is a balance-due notice. The IRS sends reminders if the balance remains unpaid, and later notices describe stronger collection steps.

Do I owe interest if I pay on time?

The IRS page says no interest is charged if you pay the full amount by the notice date. Interest builds on unpaid amounts after that.

Can I get the penalty removed?

Some penalties can be reduced or removed for reasonable cause or through administrative relief. See the IRS penalty relief pages and your notice for what applies.

What if I already paid?

Call the number on the notice and give the payment date and method. Keep your proof of payment in case the IRS asks for it.

Do I need a professional?

Not necessarily. A CP14 is designed to be handled directly. A tax professional can help if the figures are complicated.
