CP2000 response letter sample: when part of the proposed change is wrong

Last updated 2026-11-02 · Sample response letters
Short answer

A CP2000 response letter identifies the notice, goes through each proposed item, says what you accept and what you dispute with a reason, lists your enclosures, and is signed. Use the response form and reply option your notice describes, and send the letter with it by the date on your notice.

When this letter fits

A CP2000 proposes changes to your return because income or payment information that others reported to the IRS does not match what is on the return. The IRS says it is not a bill, and the change may increase, decrease or leave your tax unchanged. This sample suits a common middle case: some of the listed items are right, some are duplicates or belong to someone else, and you want to explain item by item.

Picture a self-employed designer who receives a CP2000 listing three payers. One payer's 1099 was reported on the return, a second was combined into a single line, and the third form carries her Social Security number but is for a different person's work. The letter walks through each one in order.

Read your notice before you adapt anything

Your notice tells you how to reply: the response form, the IRS document upload tool, a fax number or the mailing address at the top left. It also gives the date by which to reply; the IRS gives no standard day count, so write nothing from memory and use the date on your notice. Publication 5181 describes the choices as agree or disagree, so complete and sign the response form to match what you decided and attach this letter as your explanation.

If you need more time, the IRS says you can ask by mail, fax or the toll-free number on the notice. If you neither respond nor ask, additional tax could be assessed and interest keeps accruing, and the IRS may later send a notice of deficiency with a 90-day period to petition the Tax Court that cannot be extended.

How to adapt it

Keep each item in the order the notice lists it, and use the notice's own wording for the payer name and amount. State what you accept without argument, because a clear concession on one line makes your explanation of the other lines easier to follow.

Describe documents rather than characterizing people. "The enclosed bank statement shows the deposit was a reimbursement" is better than an accusation. If you need a record you do not have, an IRS wage and income transcript can show what payers reported.

What to attach

Enclose copies, never originals, and label each one with the item number it supports.

  • The signed CP2000 response form
  • A copy of the first page of the notice
  • The return pages where the item was reported
  • The payer's corrected form, if one was issued
  • Bank or ledger records tying deposits to the item
  • A short written note for each duplicated or misattributed form

Sample response letter
[YOUR FULL NAME]
[YOUR STREET ADDRESS]
[CITY, STATE ZIP]
[DAYTIME PHONE]

[DATE]

Internal Revenue Service
[ADDRESS FROM TOP LEFT OF YOUR NOTICE]

RE: Notice [NOTICE NUMBER] dated [NOTICE DATE]
Tax year: [TAX YEAR]
Taxpayer identification number (last four digits): [LAST FOUR OF SSN]
Response reference or case number, if shown: [REFERENCE NUMBER]

To the Reviewer:

I am responding to the notice referenced above, which proposes changes to my [TAX YEAR] return. I have reviewed each item and I am enclosing the signed response form and supporting records. I agree with part of the proposed change and disagree with part of it, as explained below.

Item 1: [PAYER NAME 1], amount shown on notice [AMOUNT FROM NOTICE]
I agree with this item. It was reported on my return, but on a combined line, and I accept that it should be shown separately. See enclosure 2.

Item 2: [PAYER NAME 2], amount shown on notice [AMOUNT FROM NOTICE]
I disagree with this item. [DESCRIBE WHY, FOR EXAMPLE: THE SAME PAYMENT WAS REPORTED ON TWO FORMS FROM THE PAYER.] The enclosed records show [DESCRIBE DOCUMENT AND WHAT IT SHOWS]. See enclosures 3 and 4.

Item 3: [PAYER NAME 3], amount shown on notice [AMOUNT FROM NOTICE]
I disagree with this item. The form carries my taxpayer identification number, but the payment was made to [DESCRIBE WHO RECEIVED IT]. I have asked the payer to issue a corrected form. See enclosure 5.

Based on the above, I ask that you reconsider the proposed change and send me a written reply describing any remaining differences. If you need more information, please contact me at the number above.

Sincerely,

[SIGNATURE]
[YOUR FULL NAME]
[DATE SIGNED]

Enclosures:

2. [DESCRIBE ITEM: RETURN PAGE OR SCHEDULE]
3. [DESCRIBE ITEM: PAYER STATEMENT OR CONTRACT]
4. [DESCRIBE ITEM: BANK STATEMENT PAGES]
5. [DESCRIBE ITEM: CORRECTED FORM REQUEST OR PAYER LETTER]
6. Copy of notice, page 1

Frequently asked questions

Does the IRS accept a letter in place of the response form?

The notice tells you what to use. If a response form is included, the IRS says to complete and sign it stating whether you agree or disagree and attach your support. Use the letter as an attachment.

Do I need to file an amended return if I agree?

The IRS page says that if you agree and there are no other changes, you follow the notice's instructions and do not need to file an amended return. If you have other changes, read the notice for what to do.

How do I send the letter?

By the IRS document upload tool, by fax to the number on the notice, or by mail to the address at the top left of the notice.

What if the payer will not correct the form?

Say so in your letter, describe your attempts, and enclose whatever records show the real facts. Check your notice for next steps.
