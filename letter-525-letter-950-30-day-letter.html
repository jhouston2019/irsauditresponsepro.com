Letter 525 and Letter 950: what a 30-day letter asks of you

Last updated 2026-11-02 · IRS notices and letters
Short answer

Letter 525 and Letter 950 are 30-day letters sent after an audit. They propose changes to your return and let you either agree or request an appeal by the date printed on the letter. Smaller disputes can use a short written request; larger ones need a formal protest.

First, a note on sources

Unlike many IRS notices, these two letters do not have their own explanatory page on irs.gov. The IRS lists them on its Appeals page for letters and notices that offer an appeal opportunity, and that list is the main official description. This page therefore sticks to what that list and the IRS appeal publications say, and it leaves the details of your case to the letter in your hands.

What the two letters are

The Appeals page describes Letter 525 as a general 30-day letter that proposes adjustments to a return and gives you the choice to agree or appeal. Letter 950 is listed as a 30-day letter for a straight deficiency, which can cover an unagreed deficiency, an overassessment or a mix of both. In both cases the letter normally arrives after an examination has ended without agreement.

The name comes from the response time: the IRS pages show the window as 30 days from the date of the letter. Treat the date printed on your letter as controlling. If it is different from what you read here, the letter wins.

What is usually enclosed

A 30-day letter generally comes with an examination report that lays out the proposed changes, by tax period, and the resulting tax and penalties. It may also include the IRS booklet on your appeal rights, Publication 5, which explains small case requests and protests. Read the report line by line against your own records before you decide anything. The report is the clearest statement of what the examiner changed and why.

Your three paths

After reading the report, you generally end up in one of these places:

  • Agree. Sign and return the agreement form that came with the letter, following its instructions, and the proposed changes go forward. Interest and penalties are part of the balance, so check the totals first.
  • Disagree and request an appeal. Appeals is a separate part of the IRS that reviews examination disputes without going to court, and asking for it does not give up your right to go to court later.
  • Do nothing. Not responding by the date on the letter means you have not agreed and have not asked for an appeal, so read your letter closely for what it says happens next, and consider asking for more time before the date rather than after.

Small case request or formal protest

Publication 5 draws a line based on the amounts in your letter. A small case request is available when the total tax and penalties for each tax period in the letter is $25,000 or less. It can be a brief statement or the form included with the letter. If any period is above $25,000 then a formal written protest is required, and you protest all periods. A formal protest is also needed for employee plan, exempt organization, partnership and S corporation cases regardless of amount.

A formal protest includes your statement that you want to appeal, your name, address and daytime phone, a list of the issues and periods you dispute, the facts supporting your position, the law or authority you rely on, and a statement made under penalties of perjury. Send either kind of request to the address on the letter, not directly to Appeals, by the date in the letter.

A worked example of getting organized

Picture a self-employed carpenter whose Letter 525 disallows a block of tool and supply expenses. He pulls the examination report, marks each disallowed line, and writes next to each one which receipts, bank statements or invoices relate to it. Two of the lines he decides to accept; for the rest he plans to appeal. That sorting, line by line, turns a general sense of unfairness into a list of specific issues, which is exactly what a small case request or protest needs.

Frequently asked questions

Is Letter 525 the same as Letter 950?

No. Both are 30-day letters offering an appeal, but the IRS Appeals list describes Letter 525 as a general 30-day letter and Letter 950 as one for a straight deficiency. Your letter's own text tells you what applies.

Does the 30 days start when I receive the letter?

The IRS pages describe the response time as 30 days from the letter date. Use the date printed on your letter, and ask for more time early if you need it.

Do I have to go to Appeals?

No. Using Appeals is optional. Agreeing, requesting an appeal or letting the process continue are all choices, and requesting an appeal does not waive the right to go to court.

Where do I send my appeal request?

To the address in the letter. The IRS says to send the request to that address rather than to Appeals directly.

Can I get a copy of Publication 5?

Yes. It is available on irs.gov and should also have come with your letter.
