CP11 notice: the IRS changed your return and your balance

Last updated 2026-11-02 · IRS notices and letters
Short answer

A CP11 tells you the IRS corrected mistakes on your return and the amount you owe is now different. If you agree, you pay, and a payment plan may be possible. If you disagree, contact the IRS at the number on the notice before the date printed on it.

What the notice is telling you

A CP11 arrives when the IRS has found mistakes on a return it processed and has changed the figures. As a result, the balance you owe is different from what you calculated. The notice explains what was changed, so it works like a corrected statement sitting beside your original return.

Picture someone who filed a simple return with a credit entered on the wrong line, or whose subtotal did not add up. The IRS fixes it, recomputes the total, and mails the CP11 to show the new amount due. Your situation may look different, but the principle is the same: the IRS changed the return and wants you to review the change.

Check their math against yours

Before you pay or object, lay your copy of the filed return beside the notice. Go line by line through the changes the IRS lists. It helps to write a short list with three columns: what you filed, what the IRS says it should be, and which document supports your number.

Often the explanation is straightforward, such as a figure that was copied from the wrong place. Sometimes it is not, and that is where your own records matter. Pull the forms and statements you used when you prepared the return so you can compare them to the figures in question.

If you agree

If the correction is right, the next step is to pay what the notice asks by the date on it. Unpaid balances accrue interest, so waiting generally adds cost.

If you cannot pay in full, a payment plan may be an option. The IRS describes plan types that include paying now, a short-term plan, and a long-term monthly plan. Many individuals can apply online if they meet the IRS requirements, and fees depend on the plan type and how you pay. Those thresholds and fees change, so check the IRS page for the current figure before you apply. Penalties and interest continue until the balance is paid.

If you disagree

The instruction on a CP11 is to call the phone number on the notice before the date printed on it. Have your return, the notice and your supporting documents in front of you when you call, and write down the name or ID of anyone you speak with, the date, and what was said.

If the representative asks for paperwork, send it as instructed. The IRS notes that if support is not received, it may send the case to audit, so make sure whatever you send clearly ties to the lines in dispute.

Why the date on the notice matters

The notice gives no fixed number of days. It gives a date. According to the IRS page, if you miss it you lose the formal right to have the changes reversed, including a Tax Court appeal, although the IRS still considers information you send later.

So the practical rule is simple: if you plan to disagree, make the call early rather than at the end of the period.

Frequently asked questions

Is a CP11 a bill?

It tells you the amount you owe has changed and asks you to pay or contact the IRS by the date on the notice. Read the notice for the exact amount and instructions.

Can I pay over time?

A payment plan may be possible. The IRS describes pay-now, short-term and long-term monthly options, and interest and penalties continue until you pay. Check the IRS payment plan page for current requirements and fees.

What if I miss the date on the notice?

The IRS page says you lose the formal right to have the changes reversed, including Tax Court appeal, but the IRS still considers information you send afterward. Do not treat that as a reason to wait.

Do I have to write a letter to disagree?

The notice directs you to call the number printed on it before the date shown. A letter may help you present your reasons, but follow what your notice says first.

Does a CP11 mean I am being audited?

The notice says the IRS corrected mistakes on your return. The IRS page does mention that a case may go to audit if requested support is not received, so respond to any request for documents.
