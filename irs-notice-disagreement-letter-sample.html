Letter to the IRS disagreeing with a notice: a general template

Last updated 2026-11-02 · Sample response letters
Short answer

A disagreement letter identifies the notice by number and date, lists which items you dispute and why, points to enclosed documents, and asks for a written reply. Use the response method and date printed on the notice, and keep the tone factual and short.

A general-purpose template

Many IRS notices propose a change or state an amount. The IRS says that if you disagree with a notice, you follow its instructions and include supporting documents. This template is not tied to one notice type, so it works as a starting point when your notice does not come with a specific response form or when you want a written record alongside one.

It is deliberately plain. Think of a retiree who gets a notice saying a pension distribution was not reported, when in fact the amount was reported on a different line; the letter points to the line and encloses the page.

Because the template is general, begin by deciding what kind of response your notice actually wants. Some notices include a response form to complete and sign, some offer a phone number, and some tell you to reply in writing. This letter fits the last case, or serves as a cover letter that goes with a form. If your notice offers a form, complete it as well and refer to it in the letter.

Before you write

Read the notice for three things: the response date, how to reply, and the section that explains how the IRS arrived at its figure. Match each number in that explanation with your return and your records, and note which ones differ.

If the notice is from a specific series, such as a CP2000, read the IRS page for that notice number first, because it may require a particular form. Use this template to supplement that, not replace it.

Make a simple two-column worksheet for yourself: on the left, what the notice says; on the right, what your records say. Differences become your disputed items, and matches become the items you accept. Working this way keeps the letter honest, because you may find that part of the notice is correct and should be acknowledged.

Tone and content

Say what you dispute and why, in order. Do not guess at the IRS's reasoning. Do not include information the notice did not ask for, such as unrelated income or personal history. If you are unsure whether to concede a point, describe the evidence and let it speak.

Number your enclosures and refer to them by number in the letter, so the reviewer can move between the sentence and the paper that supports it. Write the notice number and tax year on every enclosed page, in case pages become separated. Finally, read the finished letter aloud once; if any sentence sounds like an argument rather than a fact, rewrite it.

You may ask for more time if you need it. The IRS describes extension requests for some notices; check yours for the method. Ignoring a notice can lead to further notices and, depending on type, additional tax being assessed.

A useful test for each disputed item is whether a stranger could verify it from the enclosed paper alone. If the answer is no, add the missing record or narrow the claim. Avoid legal citations and conclusions about what the law requires; stick to what happened, what the documents show and what you ask the IRS to do.

Enclosure ideas

Choose records that go straight to the disputed item.

  • The page of the notice showing the disputed amount
  • The return page or schedule where the item appears
  • Third-party documents such as statements or forms
  • A timeline of events if the question is about timing
  • Anything else the notice specifically asks you to send

Mailing it

Reply by the method your notice names. For mail, use something that produces a receipt, and keep a full copy. After mailing, watch for the IRS reply and read it carefully, since it may accept your position, partly accept it, or ask for more. If it asks for more, answer by the new date and keep the exchange in one folder. If you disagree with the IRS response, the reply will usually explain what options follow, so read that section before taking another step.

Sample letter

This is a general template with placeholders. Replace everything in square brackets with your own details, and check it against the instructions on your notice.

[YOUR FULL NAME]
[YOUR STREET ADDRESS]
[CITY, STATE ZIP]
[DAYTIME PHONE]

[DATE]

Internal Revenue Service
[ADDRESS PRINTED ON YOUR NOTICE]

RE: Notice [NOTICE NUMBER], dated [NOTICE DATE]
Tax year or period: [TAX YEAR]
Taxpayer identification number (last four digits): [LAST FOUR]

To Whom It May Concern:

I received the notice referenced above and I disagree with the following. I have written this letter to explain my position and have enclosed documents that support it.

Disputed item 1: [DESCRIBE ITEM AS STATED IN THE NOTICE]
The notice states [WHAT THE NOTICE SAYS]. I believe this is incorrect because [PLAIN-LANGUAGE REASON]. The enclosed [DESCRIBE DOCUMENT] shows [WHAT IT SHOWS].

Disputed item 2: [DESCRIBE ITEM, OR DELETE THIS SECTION]
The notice states [WHAT THE NOTICE SAYS]. I disagree because [REASON]. See enclosure [NUMBER].

Items I do not dispute: [LIST ANY ITEMS YOU ACCEPT, OR DELETE THIS LINE].

Please review the enclosed information and send me a written reply stating whether the notice will be revised. If anything further is needed, I can be reached at the phone number above or at the address shown.

Thank you for your attention.

Sincerely,

[SIGNATURE]

Frequently asked questions

Will writing a letter stop the IRS from acting?

Not automatically. Reply by the date on the notice, in the way it describes, and read the notice for what happens next. This page cannot say what the IRS will do on your case.

What if I agree with part of it?

Say so. A clear acceptance on one item helps the reviewer focus on what remains.

Do I need a certified letter?

A receipt for your mailing is useful evidence. Under the timely-mailing rule, a postmark on or before the due date generally counts, but the sender bears the risk if it is late or unreadable.

Can someone else sign for me?

Only with proper authorization. Check the IRS pages on Form 2848 and Form 8821.
